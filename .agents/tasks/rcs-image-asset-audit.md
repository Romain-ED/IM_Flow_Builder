# RCS image-asset audit

## Summary
The Domino set is not phone/load-time optimized. Its 60 tracked image files total **69.18 MB**; 46 are referenced by source and total **64.65 MB**. Nineteen referenced Domino JPEGs are 2528×1696 and 2.44–3.92 MB each, despite being displayed as message/card media. The smaller 675×450 product images (51–136 KB) are much closer to a practical mobile target. No exact duplicate image hashes were found.

## Conclusions and recommendations
The 2528×1696 Domino files are the clear load-time risk: one carousel/card image can cost ~3 MB before transport overhead, and a multi-card flow can transfer tens of MB. The 675×450 files have suitable 3:2 aspect ratio and practical byte size; the 800×550 and 786×160 files are also reasonable. The 2528×1696 files are ~1.49:1 (not intrinsically unsuitable), but are excessive resolution for a ~360–430 CSS-pixel phone card.

Guidance here is **practical heuristic, not a verified RCS platform limit**: generate card media near its rendered width (roughly 600–900 px source width), preserve the intended 3:2/16:9-ish crop, and target roughly 100–300 KB per image (preferably WebP/AVIF where the delivery path supports it; otherwise quality-controlled JPEG). Re-encode/downscale the Domino 2528×1696 set, especially `menu-pizzas-singapore.jpg`, all `store-*.jpeg`, and pizza/order screenshots; remove or archive confirmed-unused uploads after runtime verification. Add a build/CI asset budget check (for example, warn above 500 KB and fail above 1 MB for message media), and verify the live RCS provider’s accepted formats/limits separately before production use.
